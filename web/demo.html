When this gets it wrong

No detector is perfect, and it's dishonest to pretend otherwise.

  • Real people get challenged. Privacy browsers, locked-down extensions, and unusual hardware can look automated. A challenge is a second chance, never a verdict of guilt.
  • Some bots get through. A well-funded, human-piloted automation can pass. The goal is to raise the cost of abuse, not to claim a perfect wall.
  • It reads signals, not identity. It never sees who you are — only how your browser behaves.

What this page collects

  • Standard browser properties (user-agent, screen, a rendering fingerprint) plus how the mouse/keys moved during scoring.
  • The TLS & HTTP-2 handshake your browser already sends to every site — read server-side to compute a fingerprint.
  • Held only in memory for this short session, then dropped. No accounts, no cross-site tracking, no third parties.

humanymous Pass — the challenge layer

When a session scores ambiguous (not clearly human, not clearly a bot), the engine doesn't just block — it asks you to prove it with a quick interactive puzzle. humanymous Pass is built so a real person clears it by eye in seconds while an automated agent, reasoning over a static screenshot, cannot: the answer isn't in the page, it's whatever placement your intuition finds, re-simulated on the server. This is a non-blocking research surface — a red team can attack it and a blue team hardens it in a continuous wargame.
