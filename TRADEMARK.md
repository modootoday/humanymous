# Trademark & brand-use policy

The **Apache License 2.0** under which humanymous's *code* is released (see `LICENSE`)
governs copyright and patents. It does **not** grant any right to use the project's
**name, logos, or brand** — Apache-2.0 §6 says so explicitly. This short policy states
what you may and may not do with the "humanymous" marks. It follows the spirit of common
open-source trademark policies and does not restrict any right the code license gives you.

## The marks

"**humanymous**", the humanymous wordmark, and the logos/marks under `docs/assets/brand/`
are trademarks of the humanymous Authors.

## What you may do (no permission needed)

- **Use the code** under the Apache License 2.0 — run it, modify it, redistribute it,
  embed it in open or closed products.
- **State factual, nominative references** — "built with humanymous", "compatible with
  humanymous", "a fork of humanymous" — as long as it is truthful and does not imply
  endorsement, affiliation, or that your product *is* humanymous.
- **Keep the marks in unmodified copies** you redistribute.

## What needs prior written permission

- Using "humanymous" (or a confusingly similar name) as the **name of your own product,
  service, company, or a modified distribution** — a substantially modified fork should
  ship under its own name.
- Using the **logos/marks** in a way that suggests official status, endorsement, or
  affiliation, or altering them.
- Using the marks on **merchandise**, in **domain names**, or in advertising that implies
  a relationship that does not exist.

## Why forks rename

The marks exist so users can trust that "humanymous" means the code the Authors publish.
A fork is free to take the code (that is the whole point of Apache-2.0) but should not
present itself *as* humanymous — rename it, and you may say it is "derived from humanymous".
